# Finding a therapist, coach, or both

Use this when the user asks for outside support. Prepare useful material before
asking for missing search constraints. This guide is not a provider endorsement.

## Compare arrangements

| Arrangement | Potential benefit | Clarify in consultation |
|---|---|---|
| Licensed therapist who includes goal and performance work | One relationship can address emotional life and chosen practical change | Actual training, experience, session structure, and comfort with both needs |
| Therapist plus a separate coach | Distinct expertise and time for each purpose | Cost, conflicting advice, respective scope, and whether the user wants any coordination |
| Coach with a referral relationship | Focused work on goals within coaching scope | How they recognize needs beyond competence; coaching alone may not cover the requested care |

These are arrangements to compare, not clinical recommendations. Modality names
alone do not establish competence or interpersonal fit. Verify claimed credentials.

## Prepare a private brief

Use [the template](../templates/support/provider-brief.template.md). Separate
the user's stated priorities, assistant suggestions for discussion, and unknowns.
An overview can include what brings them, how they like feedback, topics they want
help with, strengths/supports they choose to mention, and what has felt unhelpful.
Avoid sending a full archive as the default introduction.

Useful consultation questions:

- How would you balance emotional exploration with practical goal work?
- What would our first few sessions look like, and how would we decide whether it helps?
- How do you respond when your interpretation doesn't fit my experience?
- What training and experience support the approaches you use? When do you switch or refer?
- How are between-session tasks, costs, cancellations, privacy, and communication handled?

These are optional questions for a human consultation, not a chat intake checklist.
[NIMH's psychotherapy overview](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/psychotherapies)
describes approaches and questions about provider qualifications, goals, and progress.

## Search only with enough practical context

For a shortlist, establish where the person will physically attend sessions,
in-person versus remote, budget/insurance, and any essential preferences. Ask only
for missing information. A timezone, old address, or travel mention is insufficient.

For US telehealth, cross-state authorization varies. Verify the provider can serve
the user in the actual session location; do not assume a home-state license covers
travel. Use current official rules for that jurisdiction. [HHS](https://telehealth.hhs.gov/licensure/licensing-across-state-lines)
explains licensing pathways and checking patient location. Outside the US, research
the relevant regulator rather than extrapolating US rules.

Use neutral queries about location, training, practice focus, and logistics. Do
not include private names, journal passages, or inferred conditions. A comparison
should show each candidate's source, claimed training, official license check when
available, fit rationale, costs, availability status, and date checked. Mark unknowns
explicitly. A directory listing is a lead; it does not verify licensure or openings.

## Support the handoff

The user chooses the provider and what to disclose. A locally prepared brief is
a draft, not permission to send. Obtain explicit authorization before contacting,
booking, or sharing; minimize the disclosed material. Do not claim that an
appointment, insurance coverage, or provider availability is confirmed without evidence.
Reuse existing authorization within its scope. Conditional permission still needs
an identified recipient and a concrete disclosure; resolve those details rather
than asking for the same general permission again.

After a consultation, capture the user's experience and any agreed role for this
system. Never label the provider good or bad solely because their interpretation
agrees or disagrees with the assistant. The [ICF referral guide](https://coachingfederation.org/resource/referring-a-client-to-therapy/)
addresses professional limits and referral rather than treating coaching as interchangeable
with therapy.
