# Pre-Commitment Conversations

> Eight structured conversations to stress-test the concrete dimensions of a long-term partnership. Based on John & Julie Gottman's *Eight Dates* with additions from Eli Finkel (NYT).
>
> Pair with [values-clarification.md](values-clarification.md): values build the compass, these conversations test whether the compass points at the same real-world life.

---

## Who This Is For

Couples considering deeper commitment (moving in, marriage, kids, long-term shared planning) who want to stress-test alignment on the concrete decisions of a shared life, not just the abstract feelings.

This works equally well for couples *already* committed who want to re-ground.

---

## How to Use

- **Eight conversations.** Each one is its own evening. Don't try to do more than one in a sitting — you'll exhaust and lose depth.
- **Tempo: one per week or every two weeks.** Slower is fine. Faster than weekly usually means you're not actually integrating between.
- **Environment matters.** Phones away. Distraction-free. Out of the house if home is loaded.
- **Prep separately first.** Each conversation has solo prep questions. Don't skip them. The point is to surface your own thinking before you have to defend it.
- **Listening rules.** When your partner is talking, you listen. You don't rebut. You don't problem-solve. You ask clarifying questions. After they finish, you can respond.
- **It's okay to discover misalignment.** That's the entire point. Misalignment discovered now is data; misalignment discovered after kids/marriage/shared finances is much harder.

---

## A Note on Therapy

This exercise is designed for two partners in good faith. **It is not a substitute for couples therapy.** If these conversations consistently go badly, surface trauma, or hit something neither of you can hold together, a Gottman- or EFT-trained therapist can help.

## Optional Primer: The 36 Questions

If the two of you have been feeling emotionally distant — going through motions, not really seeing each other — consider doing **Arthur Aron's 36 Questions** before starting the Gottman conversations.

The 36 Questions are an intimacy-building exercise (different purpose than compatibility-testing). They take 45-90 minutes, escalate in depth, and are designed to generate closeness through mutual self-disclosure. Best known from the NYT Modern Love essay ["To Fall in Love With Anyone, Do This"](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/01/11/fashion/modern-love-to-fall-in-love-with-anyone-do-this.html) by Mandy Len Catron.

Use as warm-up if connection feels thin. Skip if you're already connected and want to dive into the harder material.

## Conversation 1: Trust and Commitment

> What does it mean to fully show up for each other?

### Solo prep (15 min, before the conversation)

- What does "trust" mean to me? What would shatter it?
- What does "commitment" mean to me, beyond "we're together"?
- When have I felt most trusted by a partner? Least?
- What do I need to feel safe being vulnerable?

### Together (60-90 min)

1. What does commitment look like in our day-to-day, not just in the abstract?
2. What do we each need from the other to feel chosen?
3. What would damage our trust beyond repair? What could we recover from?
4. When have we let each other down? What happened next?
5. How do we define exclusivity, emotional and physical?
6. Are there people in our lives whose presence creates ambiguity? How do we handle that?

### After

Write down: one commitment to each other you're making after this conversation. Specific, not vague.

---

## Conversation 2: Conflict

> How do we fight, and how do we repair?

### Solo prep

- How did my family of origin handle conflict? Loud, silent, withholding, explosive, fair?
- What's my default move when I feel attacked? Withdraw, escalate, fix, deflect?
- What is my partner's default? What do I do that makes their default worse?
- When has a fight made us closer? When has one wounded us?

### Together

1. What are the fights we keep having? (Most couples have 3-5 recurring patterns.)
2. What triggers escalation for each of us — tone, specific words, timing?
3. What do we each need during conflict — space, presence, validation, problem-solving?
4. Repair: how do we re-enter after a fight? Who tends to do it? Is that fair?
5. Are there topics we avoid because they always go badly? What would it take to be able to discuss them?
6. What's an off-limits move (stonewalling, name-calling, walking out, threatening to leave)?

### After

Pick one specific repair phrase or signal you both commit to using. Write it down.

---

## Conversation 3: Sex and Intimacy

> What do we want, and what do we need to say out loud that we've been afraid to?

### Solo prep

- What does intimacy mean to me beyond sex? (Affection, eye contact, being known.)
- What do I want more of? Less of?
- What have I been afraid to ask for?
- What do I think my partner wants that I'm not giving?

### Together

1. What's our intimacy life like right now? What's working, what isn't?
2. What feels good but doesn't get talked about?
3. What do we each want to ask for that feels vulnerable?
4. How do we want to handle drift — how do we name it without making it worse?
5. What does affection look like outside of sex — touch, words, presence?
6. Are there fears, history, or experiences that shape this for either of us that the other should know?

### After

Decide together: how often will you check in on this? Don't leave it for emergencies.

---

## Conversation 4: Money

> Money is a values conversation in disguise.

### Solo prep

- What did money look like growing up? Scarcity, abundance, anxiety, status?
- What's my relationship with spending vs. saving? Earning vs. enough?
- What does "financial security" mean to me, in numbers and in feeling?
- What scares me about combining (or not combining) finances?

### Together

1. How did money work in each of our families? What did we inherit, beliefs-wise?
2. What's the income picture — current, expected, who earns what, who has what saved?
3. How do we want to handle shared expenses vs. individual ones? What feels fair?
4. Big purchases — what's the threshold for needing joint agreement?
5. Debt, family help, gifting, supporting parents — are any of these in our future?
6. What does retirement look like, and at what age? Are we aligned on lifestyle ambition?
7. If one of us earns dramatically more than the other (now or later), how do we handle that without resentment or dependency?

### After

Pick one financial topic to revisit in 30 days with concrete numbers, not vibes.

---

## Conversation 5: Family

> Kids, parents, in-laws, family of origin.

### Solo prep

- Do I want children? Truly, not the answer I think I should give?
- What's my relationship with my own parents look like in 10 years? In 20?
- Where do I want my partner's family to fit in our life?
- What's a family pattern I want to break? One I want to carry forward?

### Together

1. Children: yes, no, maybe, how many, when, what if it's hard? **Don't soft-pedal this. It's the highest-stakes question on this list.**
2. If kids: how do we want to parent? What did we love and not love about how we were parented?
3. If no kids: are we both genuinely at peace with that, or is one of us hoping the other changes their mind?
4. Aging parents — what's our role going to be? Financially? Logistically?
5. In-laws and family of origin: holidays, visits, boundaries, conflict triangulation.
6. If one of us has kids already, or wants to adopt, or fertility is in question — what's that going to look like for us?

### After

If kids is a yes/no/maybe split between you, name it explicitly. Decide whether and when to revisit. Don't bury it.

---

## Conversation 6: Fun and Adventure

> What does the life we want to live actually look like, beyond duty?

### Solo prep

- What activities make me feel most alive?
- What kind of travel/adventure do I want in my life? How often?
- What do I do for pure play, no goal attached?
- What have I given up that I miss?

### Together

1. What do we love doing together? When do we have the most fun?
2. Are our default modes of relaxation compatible — and if not, how do we make space for both?
3. Travel: how much, what kind, how do we afford it, how do we handle one of us wanting more than the other?
4. Hobbies and solo time — what does each of us need to stay ourselves inside the relationship?
5. What's a dream adventure each of us has? Are we planning toward any of them?
6. As life gets more complex (kids, jobs, aging), how do we protect play?

### After

Pick one shared adventure, big or small, to schedule in the next 6 months.

---

## Conversation 7: Growth and Spirituality

> What do we believe, and how do we want to grow?

### Solo prep

- What do I believe about meaning, purpose, what life is for?
- Where am I in my own growth — what am I working on, what am I avoiding?
- What's my relationship with religion, spirituality, or secular philosophies of life?
- How do I want to be different in 10 years?

### Together

1. What do each of us believe about meaning, purpose, the bigger picture?
2. Religion or tradition — what role does it play, and what role will it play in raising kids if relevant?
3. How do we want to grow — together and individually? What support does each of us need to grow?
4. When one of us changes (career, beliefs, identity), how do we hold that without losing each other?
5. What do we want our older selves to be like? Are we becoming those people?
6. What's a growth edge each of us is currently afraid to face?

### After

Identify one way you'll actively support each other's growth in the next 90 days.

---

## Conversation 8: Dreams

> What does the life we'd be proud of look like, and are we building it?

### Solo prep

- If I had no constraints, what would my life look like in 5, 10, 20 years?
- What dream am I afraid to name out loud?
- What dream of my partner's do I admire? Which one do I quietly doubt?
- What dream of mine have I been deferring?

### Together

1. What does each of us want our life to look like in 5 years? 20?
2. Where do we want to live, long-term? Is that aligned, or is one of us compromising?
3. Career arcs — what's the ambition picture for each of us? Whose career leads, and when?
4. Dreams we've each had that we're not sure the other knows about — share them now.
5. Joint dreams: what do we want to build *together* that neither of us could build alone?
6. What are we willing to give up for each other's dreams? What are we *not* willing to give up?

### After

Write down three joint dreams you both want, and one dream you each hold separately that the other commits to supporting.

---

## After All Eight

Do not skip this.

Sit down together one more time. Discuss:

1. What surprised you across these conversations?
2. Where did you discover alignment you didn't know you had?
3. Where did you discover misalignment, and how big is it?
4. What are the *three load-bearing things* that need to keep getting talked about, not solved once?
5. When will you revisit this whole set? (Suggested: every 2-3 years, or before a major life transition.)

---

## If You Discover a Dealbreaker

It happens. The point of this exercise is not to prove you should stay together; it's to test whether you can.

If a dealbreaker surfaces — kids, location, fundamental values, ambition gap, religion — don't bury it. Don't pretend the conversation didn't happen. The conversation *is* the data.

Sit with it. Talk to a therapist together. Decide consciously rather than drifting.

## Sources

This exercise is a synthesis. The eight-conversation backbone is Gottman's; specific question sharpening borrows from Finkel and Piver; the framing of desire-vs-security threads in Perel.

- **John & Julie Gottman, *Eight Dates: Essential Conversations for a Lifetime of Love*** (2018). Primary framework. Each of the eight conversations here roughly maps to a chapter in the book. If you want the full version with case studies and research backing, read it. The Gottman Institute also publishes free pre-commitment resources at [gottman.com](https://www.gottman.com/).
- **Eli Finkel, *The All-or-Nothing Marriage*** (2017) and his NYT essay collection on what modern marriage demands. Sharper questions about expectations and effort. The "13 Questions to Ask Before Getting Married" NYT piece (Finkel-aligned) is a tighter compatibility filter.
- **Esther Perel, *Mating in Captivity*** (2006). Frames the tension between security and adventure that runs underneath Conversations 3 (sex), 6 (fun/adventure), and 8 (dreams). [estherperel.com](https://www.estherperel.com/)
- **Susan Piver, *The Hard Questions: 100 Essential Questions to Ask Before You Say "I Do"*** (2000). Older but holds up. Useful supplemental question bank if any single Gottman conversation feels thin.

## Further Reading

If a particular conversation surfaces something heavy and you want to go deeper:

- **Kids decision specifically:** Ann Davidman's *Motherhood — Is It For Me?* workbook + Merle Bombardieri's *The Baby Decision*. Both are designed for ambivalence, not advocacy.
- **Money:** Ramit Sethi's *Money Dials* concept and the *I Will Teach You to Be Rich* podcast couple-money episodes. Most couples-money conflict is about underlying values, not numbers.
- **Conflict patterns:** Gottman's *The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work* for the Four Horsemen (criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling) and how to repair them.
- **Attachment and dynamics:** Sue Johnson's *Hold Me Tight* — EFT-based, focused on attachment cycles couples get stuck in.
- **Intimacy and desire:** Esther Perel's *Mating in Captivity* and *The State of Affairs*.
