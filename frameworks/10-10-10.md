# 10-10-10

> Will I care about this in 10 minutes, 10 months, and 10 years?

**Source:** Suzy Welch, *10-10-10: A Life-Transforming Idea* (2009)

---

## Core Concept

**Short-term emotion is loud.** Anger, fear, embarrassment, and social pressure dominate the moment a decision shows up. They distort your sense of what actually matters. 10-10-10 is a quick antidote: it forces you to compare how you'll feel about the choice across three time horizons, so the immediate spike doesn't get the only vote.

The framework asks you to imagine yourself 10 minutes, 10 months, and 10 years from now, and check whether your future self would endorse the decision. Sometimes the 10-minute self is the one to listen to (genuine red flags, real urgency). More often, the 10-month or 10-year self has the clearer view, and the loudest part of your present self is reacting to noise.

The Heath brothers cite 10-10-10 in *Decisive* as one of the cleanest tools for "attaining distance" from a decision. It's fast, portable, and works in the moment.

---

## How to Do It

1. **Write down the decision.** One sentence. Specific.
2. **Ask: "How will I feel about this in 10 minutes?"** Right after I do it. The immediate aftermath.
3. **Ask: "How will I feel about this in 10 months?"** When the dust has settled. The medium-term consequences are visible.
4. **Ask: "How will I feel about this in 10 years?"** When this moment is a small piece of a much larger story.
5. **Compare the three answers.** Which time horizon should weigh most for *this* decision? They won't all agree, and they shouldn't.

The goal isn't to always pick the 10-year answer. It's to make the trade-off visible and choose deliberately.

---

## Examples

**Should I send this angry email?**
- 10 minutes: Cathartic. Vindicated.
- 10 months: Probably regret. The relationship is strained.
- 10 years: Forgotten, or remembered as a low point.
- Verdict: 10-minute self is loud. Don't send. Sleep on it.

**Should I take this job?**
- 10 minutes: Anxious about the change. Comfort of the known.
- 10 months: Either thriving or wishing I'd stayed. Real signal here.
- 10 years: One of many career steps. Probably won't define me.
- Verdict: The 10-month horizon matters most. Lean into the medium-term gut check.

**Should I have this hard conversation?**
- 10 minutes: Uncomfortable, maybe shaky.
- 10 months: Glad I did it. The thing that was building up is addressed.
- 10 years: Either a turning point or a non-event, but better than silence.
- Verdict: Short-term cost is real but small. Have the conversation.

---

## When It Works Best

- Decisions where short-term feelings are dominating: anger, fear, embarrassment, social pressure.
- Reactive moments (someone just said something, an email just landed).
- Choices where the medium and long-term cost is invisible until you imagine it.
- Anything where "future you" has a clearer view than "right-now you."

## When It's Less Useful

- Decisions where long-term reasoning is already winning and you're ignoring real present-tense costs (burnout, health, a relationship hurting *now*).
- Choices where the short-term cost is genuinely high and not just emotional discomfort.
- Decisions that need more analysis than a single pass — 10-10-10 is a check, not a full deliberation.

If the framework keeps pointing you the same direction but you're not moving, the block isn't analytical. Different tool.

---

## Integration with Daily Practice

Use within the **deep-dive** skill when working through a specific decision. 10-10-10 is fast, so it pairs well with longer frameworks: run it first to see if the choice is obvious once distance is applied, then go deeper only if the three horizons disagree in interesting ways.

Also useful inside **monologue** when the user is venting about a reactive decision (an email to send, a conversation to have, a thing to say). Naming the three time horizons can shift the register from venting to choosing.

In **end-day**, occasionally worth asking 10-10-10 retroactively about a decision the day produced. It calibrates your sense of which decisions actually mattered.

---

## Key Questions

- What is the actual decision, in one sentence?
- Which of the three time horizons is loudest right now, and is that the one I should trust?
- If I imagine my 10-year self watching this moment, what would they want me to do?
- Is the short-term cost emotional (manageable) or substantive (real)?
