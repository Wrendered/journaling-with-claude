# Values Clarification — Couple

> Build shared values for your relationship. Roughly 90 minutes joint, after both partners have completed individual pre-work.

---

## Why

Individual values tell you who **you** are. Couple values tell you what **this relationship** is for. Without them you drift; with them you have a shared compass.

The point is not to merge into one person. Each of you keeps your individual values. The couple values are what you build together.

---

## Pre-Work (Do Separately, Before Meeting)

Each person does the [individual values clarification](../exercises/values-clarification.md) — but **narrow to 4 values, not 2**. You need more material for the joint conversation.

Each person should bring:
- 4 core values, each with a personal definition (1-2 sentences in your own words)
- 3 behaviors that support each value
- 3 slippery behaviors (what you do when you're not living the value)

**Important:** Don't look at each other's lists until you sit down together. Do this part fully solo.

---

## The Joint Session (90 min)

Set aside uninterrupted time. Put phones away. Have something to write on (shared doc, paper, whiteboard).

---

### Step 1: Share (15 min)

Take turns. Each person reads their 4 values + definitions + supporting behaviors + slippery behaviors. **Uninterrupted.** The listener doesn't react, debate, or comment until the speaker is fully done.

After both have shared, ask each other:
- "What surprised you about my list?"
- "Anything you'd expect to see that wasn't there?"

Don't judge or argue. Just register.

---

### Step 2: Find Overlap (15 min)

Together, identify values that appear on both lists — OR that are named differently but mean similar things.

For each shared value:

| Shared value | Person A defines as | Person B defines as |
|--------------|---------------------|---------------------|
|              |                     |                     |
|              |                     |                     |

**Don't smooth differences.** Even shared values may have different shapes (e.g., both value "Freedom" but one means "ability to take big risks" and the other means "no obligations"). Surface the difference. Write both definitions.

---

### Step 3: Identify Divergences (15 min)

Now the other side. Values that appear on only one list.

For each:
- The person whose value it is explains: *Why does this matter to you?*
- The other person responds honestly: *Does this matter to me too, or am I neutral? Does it conflict with anything on my list?*

Some divergences are fine — you don't need to share every value. Others reveal real tension worth naming.

**Flag any divergence that creates friction.** Don't try to resolve it in this step.

---

### Step 4: Pick Your Couple Values (20 min)

From the overlap (and any divergences you both decide to adopt jointly), choose **3 shared couple values** that this relationship is built on.

Criteria:
- Both of you agree it matters
- You're willing to act on it, not just claim it
- It points at what makes *this* relationship distinctive (not generic relationship goals)

Your 3 couple values:

1. _____________
2. _____________
3. _____________

For each, write together (1-2 sentences each):
- What this value means **in our relationship** (your shared definition)
- A specific time you lived it well together
- A specific time you drifted from it

---

### Step 5: Operationalize (20 min)

For each couple value:

**Behaviors that support it**
- What does each of us do?
- What do we do together?

**Slippery patterns**
- What does drift look like in this relationship specifically?
- What's the early warning sign — what behavior, what tone, what avoidance?

**Repair mechanism**
- When we drift, how do we name it without blame?
- Pick a phrasing both of you agree to. Suggestion: *"I think we're slipping on [value]."* Neutral, no accusation, opens a conversation rather than a fight.

---

### Step 6: Disagreements & Tensions (10 min — don't skip)

Name explicitly:

- **Values one of you holds that the other doesn't share** — is there a workable arrangement? Does the non-sharer at least *respect* the value enough not to undermine it?
- **Tensions between values** — e.g., one of you holds "adventure," the other holds "security." Both are real. How do you hold both? What does compromise look like, and what does it cost each of you?

This step is where the exercise pays for itself. The point is not to eliminate tensions — most couples have them — but to **know what they are** so you stop fighting about them blind.

---

## After

- Store the output somewhere both of you can see it.
- Re-read together every 3 months.
- Redo the full exercise every 1-2 years.

---

## Caveats

- This works best when both partners did the pre-work in good faith.
- If you find yourselves arguing rather than discussing, pause. The exercise is not the place to win a fight — it's the place to map where each of you stands.
- If you discover a values misalignment that feels load-bearing for the relationship, that's data. The exercise didn't create it; it surfaced it.
- It's okay if this takes longer than 90 minutes. Don't rush Step 6.
