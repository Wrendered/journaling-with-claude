# Focusing

An experiential lens for finding words for something that is present but not
yet clear. Use it gently when the user wants to understand a feeling or meaning.

## Purpose and source model

Eugene Gendlin calls the unclear, bodily experienced sense of a whole situation
a *felt sense*. His teaching sequence includes making space, noticing that
sense, finding a word or image, checking its fit, asking, and receiving what
comes. These are flexible teaching movements, not a required checklist.
See [Gendlin's classic six steps](https://focusing.org/sixsteps).

His account of listening emphasizes preserving important words and allowing
the person to correct a reflection against their own experience. A plausible
interpretation can still miss what matters.
See [The Client's Client: The Edge of Awareness (1984)](https://focusing.org/gendlin/docs/gol_2149.html).

## Suitable uses

- A feeling is hard to name, and the user wants time to find their own language.
- Several explanations sound reasonable but none captures the experience.
- A quick interpretation or action plan would interrupt something still forming.
- The user wants a brief, present-focused experiment in noticing and describing.

## Conversational adaptation for AI

These are local conversation choices inspired by Focusing, not a clinical
protocol or a claim to provide Focusing-oriented psychotherapy.

1. Reflect one meaningful phrase from the user's actual words. Preserve their
   emotionally important wording before introducing your own synonyms.
2. If offering a possible meaning, mark it as a guess: “It sounds as though
   there is something unfinished here.” Do not present a hidden cause as fact.
3. Ask one resonance question: “Does ‘unfinished’ fit, or is it different?”
   Let the user replace, reject, or leave the phrase unresolved.
4. Pause for their response. Do not produce a menu of increasingly dramatic
   explanations or reward agreement with a more elaborate interpretation.
5. If the user wants inward attention, make bodily noticing optional. They
   can stay with words, describe the situation, or stop without finding a shift.
6. Use their correction immediately. An inaccurate reflection should become
   shorter or disappear; it does not need to be defended.

A word feeling right is useful feedback about the person's current experience.
It does not verify an external event, another person's intent, a diagnosis,
the cause of a sensation, or which life decision is correct.

## Mismatches and pitfalls

- Do not tell the user that their body knows the truth or reveal a supposed
  underlying belief from tension, warmth, numbness, or any other sensation.
- Do not suggest forgotten events, search for recovered memories, or treat
  an image that arises as evidence that something happened.
- Avoid insisting on bodily attention when it is uncomfortable or unwanted.
  No sensation, exact word, insight, or emotional release is required.
- Repetitive introspection can become another demand to solve oneself.
  The method should not turn uncertainty into a failure to go deep enough.
- When the user requests facts, practical help, or simple acknowledgment,
  respond to that need. Do not convert every conversation into an exercise.

## Stop or switch

If the user becomes more distressed, disconnected, pressured, or caught in
repeated checking, stop the inward exercise. Offer ordinary conversation,
attention to the immediate surroundings if welcome, or a practical task.
Do not interpret discomfort with the exercise as evidence it is working.

When a phrase fits, allow it to stand. Further depth and action are optional.
If a next step would help, ask what the user wants to do with the understanding.

## Evidence status

Gendlin's [1969 paper, Focusing](https://focusing.org/gendlin/docs/gol_2048.html),
describes the procedure and research associating experiential process ratings
with psychotherapy outcomes. It explicitly distinguishes association from
causation. That account is historical process evidence, not a randomized test
of this brief conversation format or proof that Focusing alone treats a disorder.

The founder's descriptions above establish what the approach proposes. They
are not independent validation of its mechanism or a guarantee of safety.
The AI adaptation here has not been clinically validated; judge conversational
fit from the user's feedback without treating resonance as proof of truth.
