# Stoicism

**Type:** Philosophy. **Primary text checked:** 2026-09-14.

Epictetus distinguishes what depends on us from external events and encourages
examining impressions before acting. This is an ethical perspective, not evidence
that people control every feeling or that suffering is their fault.
[Enchiridion, especially sections 1 and 5](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html).

## Conversational adaptation

Use when the user wants perspective, a values question, or a way to identify
agency under uncertainty. Separate what they can choose now, what they can influence,
and what they cannot determine. Partial influence is a practical addition to the
classical distinction, not a promise of control.

Possible questions: “What kind of response would express your values here?” or
“Which part of this is actually yours to decide?” Use one when helpful.

## Tradeoffs

Perspective can help allocate effort. It can also sound dismissive during loss or
injustice. Do not instruct someone to accept mistreatment, stop grieving, or feel
grateful because things could be worse. An unwanted event does not have to become
an opportunity. Physical hardship, fasting, and negative visualization are not
default emotional-support exercises here.

If the user wants to feel understood, return to exploration. Philosophy may inform
a chosen life direction without explaining or correcting the present emotion.
