# Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT)

**Purpose:** Combine acknowledgment of present experience with skills for choosing
what to do next. The adaptation here supports emotional pauses, clear requests,
and reflection on responses in everyday conversation.

## Model and assumptions

Marsha Linehan developed DBT as a clinical treatment. Standard DBT includes
individual therapy, skills training, between-session coaching, and a therapist
consultation team. The four skills areas are mindfulness, distress tolerance,
emotion regulation, and interpersonal effectiveness. See Linehan's
[2025 publisher excerpt](https://www.guilford.com/excerpts/linehan6_preface_ch2.pdf?t=1).

Acceptance and change can both matter: an experience can make sense in context,
and a different response may help. This is a useful stance, not evidence that
people suffer because they refuse reality or lack effort. A skill may be missing,
hard to access, poorly matched, or insufficient for the situation.

“Wise Mind” is a practice metaphor for considering emotion and reasoning together.
It does not establish a hidden correct answer, nor make strong emotion an inferior
source of information.

## When this lens fits

- The user wants a pause before acting on an intense urge.
- A request, boundary, disagreement, or repair needs concrete wording.
- The user wants to understand the sequence leading to an unwanted response.
- An unchangeable fact needs acknowledgment alongside decisions still available.

These conversational uses are design choices. Do not turn every strong feeling
into a regulation problem. Sometimes the useful response is listening, changing
the environment, or obtaining practical support.

## What AI can borrow conversationally

Offer one skill that fits the moment, in ordinary language. Expand its acronym
only when that helps the user remember it.

**Pause (adapted from STOP):** Stop the immediate response, take a step back,
observe what is happening, and choose the next move deliberately. In chat this
might mean helping the user postpone sending a draft and identify what it needs
to accomplish. A pause need not make the feeling disappear.

**Clarify a request (adapted from DEAR MAN):** Describe the relevant facts, express
the impact, assert the request, explain its benefit, stay on topic, communicate
steadily, and negotiate where appropriate. Help draft language that sounds like
the user. Consider their objective, relationship, and self-respect; a clear
request cannot guarantee agreement or safety.

**Check facts and options:** Distinguish observations from interpretations while
validating the emotion. Ask what the urge would do in this actual context.
Opposite action is an option to consider when the urge is mismatched or unhelpful;
approaching danger or disclosing to an unsafe person is not a default exercise.

**Map a recent sequence:** With the user's interest, identify the event, relevant
conditions, interpretations, emotion, urge, action, and consequence. Look for one
place where support or a different response was possible. Include consequences
and accountability without treating the map as proof of causation.

**Acceptance with agency:** Name what has happened and what remains open to
influence. Acceptance never requires approval, forgiveness, tolerating abuse, or
abandoning change. Avoid prompts that presume the user is “refusing to accept.”

## Pitfalls and limits

- Avoid blaming distress on insufficient acceptance, poor sleep, or absent skills.
  Physical needs and external pressures deserve curiosity, not moral judgment.
- This adaptation does not prescribe TIPP/TIP cold-water, breath-holding, or intense
  exercise procedures. Do not promise a nervous-system reset or offer physiological
  treatment instructions. If the user already has a clinician-taught plan, help
  them recall that plan without inventing substitutions.
- Quiet attention or comfortable breathing can be offered as an optional pause,
  without fixed respiratory targets. If inward attention is uncomfortable, shift
  to the surroundings or ordinary conversation.
- Use regulation to widen choice, not to suppress justified anger or make someone
  more compliant. Do not infer diagnosis from intensity or interpersonal conflict.

## Evidence scope

A trial of 99 women with borderline personality disorder and high suicide risk
found similar improvements in suicide-related outcomes across three structured
DBT conditions; conditions including skills training showed advantages on some
other outcomes ([Linehan et al., 2015](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/psych/articlepdf/2205835/yoi140120.pdf)).
This was treatment with trained providers and a risk-management protocol.

Delivery changes can matter substantially. A large trial found that offering
brief online training in selected DBT skills to outpatients reporting frequent
suicidal ideation increased self-harm risk relative to usual care
([Simon et al., 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2789028)).
That result concerns the tested outreach program and population, not every DBT
skill or conversational use. It is a concrete reason not to transfer clinical
treatment claims to a reduced digital adaptation.

Neither study validates this repository or general AI chat as DBT treatment.
